WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN

A laboratory, with a reference.

The simulator tracks every card in a finite shoe. American hole-card rules, dealer peek, one-card split aces, up to four hands, and doubles on any first two cards. No insurance, side bets, bankroll sizing, or resplitting aces.

The reference samples possible hidden cards and shoe orders using only exposed information. Each legal action is evaluated with basic-strategy continuations. It is an approximate policy improvement method, not an exact optimal solver. Intervals show Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty.

Laya predicts an action preference, next-hit bust probability, and dealer finish distribution. Base model probabilities are not blackjack calibrated. Training uses simulator labels and fits temperatures on a separate validation split. Agreement with this teacher does not prove optimality.

The unseen shoe includes the dealer’s concealed card. A negative ace/ten peek changes the probabilities of the next draw. The model never sees the simulation seed, hidden card, or future deck order.

Small shoes use an early shuffle reserve. A rare mid-round exhaustion voids the whole round, returns all bets, and is recorded explicitly.
